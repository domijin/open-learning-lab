# Governance

## Decision principle

Open participation does not imply majority vote. Scarce attention, compute, expert time, and learner effort require evidence-based allocation.

A useful question is:

> What is the cheapest experiment or review that would materially update the decision?

## Proposal admission

Evidence-backed proposals receive priority. Under-evidenced but falsifiable ideas should get the benefit of the doubt when a cheap proof-of-concept can manufacture the missing evidence.

Reputation may influence how much pre-test evidence is required when review itself is costly. Reputation must **not** change how experimental results are interpreted.

## Promotion

Promote an intervention only when evidence justifies the next resource scale. A small win does not imply large-scale success; where scale matters, measure response across multiple affordable scales.

## Findings

Findings should state:
- claim;
- evidence;
- conditions tested;
- uncertainty/confidence;
- contradictions;
- decision relevance.

"Worked" is usually too weak a finding.

## Expert panel

Experts are not a standing committee that must approve everything. Route only high-information-value questions.

Preserve disagreement. Over time, expert judgment may be calibrated contextually (for example, ML architecture vs learning retention), never as one global reputation score.

## Supersession

Published research records are append-only in spirit. If a finding changes, add a new record with `supersedes` and explain why.

## Project maintainers

Maintainers are stewards of protocol quality and repository integrity, not final epistemic authorities. Evidence should be inspectable and challengeable.
