# Licensing Policy

Open Learning Lab uses a simple dual-license model.

## Software — MIT

Source code, scripts, packages, workflow code, and other software are licensed under the [MIT License](../LICENSE).

Why:
- low friction for reuse and integration;
- compatible with commercial and open-source use;
- appropriate for the project's current lightweight software surface.

## Learning and research content — CC BY 4.0

Documentation, prompts, protocols, study materials, intentionally published learning traces, research narratives, findings, and similar non-software content are licensed under [CC BY 4.0](../LICENSE-CONTENT.md), unless otherwise noted.

Why:
- permits copying, adaptation, redistribution, and commercial reuse;
- preserves attribution and provenance;
- fits open education and research better than a software license.

## Third-party material

Open Learning Lab's licenses apply only to material contributors have the right to license.

Do not assume that citing, linking, quoting, or storing metadata about an external source changes that source's license. Preserve source attribution and original license/terms.

## Contributions

By contributing material to this repository, contributors agree that their contribution may be distributed under the license applicable to the area they modify:

- software contributions -> MIT;
- non-software learning/research content -> CC BY 4.0;

unless a file or contribution explicitly states another compatible license and maintainers accept it.

## Mixed files

If a file contains both substantial software and substantial prose/content, prefer splitting it into separate files. When that is impractical, add an explicit file-level license notice.
