# Marin Study — Decision Log

This file preserves major learning-design decisions from the initiating session without publishing a raw transcript.

## D001 — Re-discover before reconstruct

**Decision:** Ask the learner to make the same class of judgment call that could have led to Marin before teaching Marin's implementation.

**Reason:** Matching terminology is weaker evidence of understanding than independently deriving the mechanism from constraints.

## D002 — Model open development through mechanisms

The learner independently summarized three candidate mechanisms:

1. **external knowledge** — diversity expands the hypothesis/search space;
2. **internal commitment** — openness creates epistemic discipline and reproducibility pressure;
3. **sustainable economy** — public legitimacy/contribution can extend the resource horizon.

These are working hypotheses, not axioms.

## D003 — Cheap evidence before expensive commitment

Under-evidenced ideas should not be rejected when a small proof-of-concept can cheaply create decision-relevant evidence. Promising interventions should then be tested across larger scales where scaling behavior matters.

## D004 — Optimize learning, not straight-line wins

Failed/regressive experiments may still have positive value when they falsify a useful hypothesis or reveal boundary conditions.

## D005 — Durable object is knowledge, not leaderboard score

A useful research record should preserve what hypothesis was tested, effect/uncertainty, conditions, contradictions, and decision relevance—not only whether a benchmark improved.

## D006 — Do not collapse causal chains

A transfer exercise in clinical MRI exposed a key guardrail: segmentation improvement -> clinical decision improvement -> patient benefit -> cost effectiveness -> social welfare are separate hypotheses. The learning system should make untested arrows visible rather than narrating them as one causal story.
